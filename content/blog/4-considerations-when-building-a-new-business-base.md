Choosing a new business base involves so much more than finding enough square footage, signing a lease and decorating. You need premises that suit the way your company runs right now, and can also accommodate its plans for the future. Consider all the ways in which [your office](https://thewellpaidexpert.com/how-to-prepare-your-office-for-the-future-of-hybrid-work) might need to support employee safety, ways of working, and long-term costs. With this in mind, here are four important considerations you should be making.

## 1. Design Features for Safety

Safety needs to be the center of attention when planning a new building. Consider how staff, visitors and deliveries will entire the site, where vehicles and pedestrians will move, and whether entrances are visible and easy to supervise. Lighting, clear sightlines, access control and maintained outdoor areas can all contribute to a property that feels secure. Think about reception areas, parking spaces and entrance after normal working hours too. This is also where [crime prevention design](https://architectureandaccess.com.au/crime-prevention-through-environmental-design-cpted-2/) can inform decisions about a building’s layout. Crime prevention through environmental design considers visibility, access and how spaces are maintained. These principles cannot prevent every incident, but can inform discussions with your architect or security advisor.

## 2. Choose a Location That Works

Your business base needs to work for the people who use the building. Think about how employees travel to work, whether customers can find the premises and how close you are to suppliers, transport links and services. If your business relies on deliveries, check loading access and any restrictions on larger vehicles. Also look at nearby amenities, as cafes, shops and other services can make the location more convenient for employees.

Visit at different times of day to check traffic, parking, noise and public transport. Consider recruitment too, because a site that is difficult to reach may limit your choice of employees, even if the building itself meets all of your needs.

## 3. Plan for the Way Your Team Works

Think about what employees need to do during their day to day. Some roles need private call spaces, and others benefit from shared meeting areas. You may need training rooms, storage and visitor facilities. Think about how departments interact, where confidential discussions take place and whether customers need a dedicated meeting space. Hybrid working also needs a level of flexibility. Not everyone will be in the building everyday, so you may not need a permanent desk for every employee. Shared workstations need suitable equipment, connectivity and enough space. Ask team members what helps them do their jobs well, then use their answers to guide the design.

## 4. Look Beyond the Initial Cost

Before signing anything, look into business rates, utilities, insurance, maintenance, cleaning, security and refurbishment. A cheaper building may need significant work before it’s ready too. Ask for a clear picture of what the [running costs](https://strategyhat.co.uk/calculator-tools/budget-calculator/?ppc_keyword=office%20fit%20out%20costs%20per%20square%20foot&gad_source=1&gad_campaignid=1477682372&gbraid=0AAAAAC-C7Owz40FFA3te6YotHL-O-1mTg&gclid=Cj0KCQiAiJXYBhD_ARIsAAx2AHn5QjgEtQZ-ibn301USi7wx40OeTjFcs0-YUSDV3XJHQuT8Wq3cP0QaAvRAEALw_wcB) might be, not just the amount due at the start of the agreement.

All in all, assess safety, location, working needs and costs before committing. Asking the right questions early on can help you avoid expensive surprises and will help you choose a property that works for now and the future.
