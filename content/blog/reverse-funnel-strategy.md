Building a reverse funnel means designing your business from the top down: your best, most expensive offer first, with intentional alternatives for people who aren't ready.

Most business builders do the opposite — they start with something cheap and hope customers upgrade. The reverse funnel skips that climb entirely.

## Start With Your Premium Anchor

Before you build anything else, you need to know what your best offer looks like. This is the thing you'd sell if price were no object and the client gave you full trust to do the work the right way.

**Questions to define your anchor:**

- What outcome do clients want most from you?
- What's the fastest, most reliable way you can deliver it?
- What would it cost a company to hire in-house for that same result?

Price your anchor at the high end of what the market can bear and what your results justify. If you're a social media strategist with proven client results, that might be a $3,000–$5,000/month retainer. If you're a consultant with CFO-level expertise, it might be a $10,000/month engagement.

The anchor price sets the psychological frame for everything below it.

## Design the Mid-Tier Alternative

Not everyone will say yes to your premium offer, and not everyone should. The mid-tier is for people who are serious but need a lower-risk entry point.

Common mid-tier formats:
- **Short-term engagement:** 90-day sprint instead of an ongoing retainer
- **Workshop or intensive:** A 1-day or 1-week focused engagement
- **Group program:** Your methods delivered to a cohort instead of 1:1
- **Lighter scope:** Fewer platforms, fewer deliverables, same quality

The mid-tier should create genuine value on its own — not just exist to funnel people up. It should also create a natural path toward the premium offer for clients who experience your work and want more.

## Create the Self-Study Base

The base of your reverse funnel is for people who can't currently afford your time but want to learn your approach.

This is where a product like a course, guide, or framework comes in. It should deliver real value — not a watered-down teaser. The goal is to give people enough to make progress, while making it clear that accelerating results with your direct involvement is available to them when they're ready.

Characteristics of a strong self-study base product:
- **Clear, specific scope** — not "everything you need to know," but "the exact system for [specific outcome]"
- **Low price relative to the value** — $27–$197 for most expert businesses
- **Built-in next step** — makes it obvious what joining the mid-tier or premium offer looks like

## The Conversation Flow

In a reverse funnel, sales conversations start at the top:

1. **Initial outreach or inquiry:** You lead with your premium offer. You ask about their situation, goals, and timeline — not their budget.
2. **Objection to price or commitment:** You acknowledge and introduce the mid-tier. "If the full retainer isn't the right fit right now, there's a 90-day intensive designed for exactly this situation."
3. **Still not ready:** You offer the self-study product as a bridge. "If you'd like to explore the methodology on your own first, I have a guide that shows the full system for $[X]."

Notice: you never push. The funnel presents options, and each option has genuine value. The client chooses their own level of commitment.

## Why This Converts Better

Several psychological principles work in your favor with this structure:

**Anchoring.** Your premium price makes the mid-tier feel like a deal, even if it's still not cheap.

**Perceived value.** Leading with premium signals that you're selective and in-demand. That's attractive. Leading with $27 signals that $27 is what you think your time is worth.

**Trust before transaction.** Clients who buy the self-study and find real value become highly qualified prospects for your premium work later.
