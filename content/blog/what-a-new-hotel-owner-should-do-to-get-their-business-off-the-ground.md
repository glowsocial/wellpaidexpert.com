## Run Hotel Centric Events

Marketing in the hospitality industry is a little different to marketing in every other industry. Not only does it require a more tangible, in person effort, but it can be much more costly as well.

Both of these things will require careful handling; check out some tips available at [https://www.gourmetmarketing.net/blog/hotel-marketing-guide](https://www.gourmetmarketing.net/blog/hotel-marketing-guide) for some crucial insight into the specific brand of marketing required for hotels.

But long story short, this will mean [organizing and running events](https://thewellpaidexpert.com/5-marketing-events-to-boost-your-business/) within your hotel, or running events that rely on your hotel. This could be something like a party, a dance night, or an open mic/poetry event, or providing a venue for a stand up comedian or a local band to perform in.

Not only does this bring in ticket sales and more booked rooms, but it ensures your hotel’s name is front and center within the local area. Running events like this once a month or every other month can be a great boost to your profits.

## Hire An Experienced Manager

A new hotel business is going to have little in the way of funds, especially compared to their better known, well established rivals. As such, you’re not going to have the same hiring capabilities. This means you’re more likely to rely on untrained and inexperienced staff, albeit those who seem a good fit during the interviews.

And this is perfectly fine; hiring the staff you can afford while maintaining a strict hiring etiquette is essential. But you should also consider hiring one member of staff who knows what they’re doing, and has plenty of experience working in a hotel environment.

This person is prime for a management position, where they can oversee the rest of the staff, make executive decisions regarding the best guest treatment, and ensure your hotel dreams come to fruition.

Knowing there is [one skilled person](https://www.testgorilla.com/blog/hotel-manager-skills/) in charge will make it much easier to focus on bringing the rest of your staff up to their level. Provide training programs for junior employees, and regularly check in to see how everyone is getting on.
