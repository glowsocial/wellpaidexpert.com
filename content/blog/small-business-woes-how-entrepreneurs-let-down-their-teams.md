Small businesses thrive when their leaders make the right decisions. But when they overlook things or get their attitude wrong, it can create massive disturbances that undermine the firm as a whole.

To compound the issue, when problems come from the top, they usually percolate throughout the entire firm, causing even more challenges.

The purpose of this post is to run through some of the ways entrepreneurs let their teams down so you can avoid them. Here’s what you need to know:

## Failure To Delegate

Entrepreneurs
often let their teams down by failing to delegate properly. Often, they recommend tasks to the wrong people, or simply fail to set out an acceptable strategy that will work.

The way to solve this is for leaders to allow other people in the organisation to step up and take ownership over specific tasks. This strategy reduces the risk of frustration, and helps workers feel like they are moving forwards in their careers and making progress.

## Ignoring Mental Health

Business leaders can also fall into the
trap of ignoring the mental health
and wellness requirements of their staff. They often believe that they need to keep working hard to make sure that they fulfill business objectives. But what they don’t realize is that most employees aren’t as motivated and committed as they are to whatever tasks they want to complete. In fact, many people simply don’t have the constitution to work much beyond forty hours a week.

To avoid mental health issues, leaders can respect work-life boundaries and also adopt a more grateful attitude towards the staff they have. This approach reduces stress in the office and also allows people to be more productive during the hours they are present.

## Micromanagement

Micromanagement is another way entrepreneurs let their teams down. Trying to do everything possible to ensure things don’t go wrong is often a recipe for resentment.

You can fix micromanagement by providing staff with more autonomy. Workers who feel that they can do the things they need to do are often
far happier being in the office
and getting down to various tasks.

## Neglecting IT Networks

Failing to implement
cyber insurance for small businesses
is another common failing of entrepreneurs. Many are so focused on the task at hand that they overlook the basics for securing the systems that keep their companies operating.

The trick here is to bring in people from the outside to do this work on behalf of the business. Managing it all internally is possible, but it is time consuming and doesn’t always work. Meanwhile, working with people outside of the firm at MSPs is often a powerful solution.

## Being Inconsistent

Finally, leaders can let down their teams by being inconsistent. Many entrepreneurs struggle to provide the same leadership characteristics day in, day out, which can leave people in their teams feeling off-balance. Sometimes they’re supportive, but other times they aren’t.

To deal with this, it helps if leaders have a clear direction. Knowing where they are going can often have a profound effect on their decision-making.
