# Freelancing? Here’s What A Website Can Do For You

Fancy freelancing as a career? Then you’re going to need a website! Freelancing was made a lot easier thanks to the popular uptake of the internet, and getting involved puts you in the right place to be discovered.

Even just the most basic of websites will propel your prospects forward! And while the primary goal of any freelancer’s website is to help them find more clients, there are a lot of other benefits involved in setting up your own online portal. Here are a few of the most noteworthy we think should be on your radar.

Pexels Image – CC0 Licence

## Showcase Your Skill

This is the most basic premise of a freelancer’s website, but it’s also one that can be missed off the list. Think of a website as
a living portfolio, and include links to your work, testimonials from clients, and snapshots of the amazing results you’ve achieved.

Remember, if your portfolio is kept offline the whole time, the only chance you’ll get to show off your skills will be when you’ve already got one foot in the door. Don’t make yourself rely on networking efforts that can be 50/50!

## Take Payment

Even freelancers can use the online business model to make money! You can use an
easy payment sdk
through your own website and start taking payments from both new and existing clients alike.

If you’ve always found invoicing to be a clunky thing you’re not too keen on, having a secure and private payment portal on your site could cut it out of your workflow entirely. Clients can log in and make use of it as and when the time comes to pay you for your services.

And if you’d like to start selling pre-packaged services, such as a blogging package if you’re a writer, or a set amount of photos and their prints if you’re a photographer, your website’s gateway will allow you to benefit from a passive ordering system.

## Build an Audience

Yes, even freelancers can
have their own audiences! You’re not just out here to create content for others looking to build their own. Start using your website to explore things your industry wants to hear, see, and experience, and you could get quite a few subscribers in a short period of time.

Even when you’re not a writer yourself, you can blog about what you do, as well as the opinions you hold. State what you feel about a current industry trend, for example, or respond to another well known figure in the niche, if they’ve put out content you have a view on.

The more you let yourself be known, the more you will be known! Combine this with some social media outreach and you could create a very dedicated following that’ll benefit your future job prospects.

If you’re a freelancer, make sure your website is doing the heavy lifting for you. You’ll bring in more leads, have a chance to rank well, and even find some fans along the way.
